In the decade-plus since Bitcoin's genesis block, technical people have done an enormous amount of work — yet self-custody itself hardly seems to have moved forward. For most non-technical users, holding and using bitcoin safely is still a heavy subject. After the 2026 Coldcard low-entropy bug, the internet has been full of fear about self-custody — and when technical people offer advice, they usually overestimate how much ordinary people know about computers and information security.

> "If you advocate for self-custody, you should also be telling people to use a multi-vendor multisig setup."
>
> — Multi-vendor multisig? No! Based on my experience, for the vast majority of people **complexity is more dangerous than hackers**, and creating a multisig wallet can be the start of a disaster. Many technical people forget how basic most people's computer skills really are — just picturing how I'd explain to your uncle that "you not only keep two keys, you also have to keep three master public keys" is enough to make me give up.

> "Satoshi never used a hardware wallet, and his coins were never stolen. Do what Satoshi did. Bitcoin Core wallet!"
>
> — Get people to babysit a `wallet.dat` file? Get them to run Bitcoin Core on an offline computer? Get them to remember to check that a newly generated address is already included in the backup? No! That's far too hard for your uncle — he deserves something simpler.

Bitcoin is a gift of our time, but its success also depends on a much broader public.

Before Bitcoin reaches the wider public, **people deserve a self-custody setup that is both safe and simple**.

Luckily, after years of trying every kind of self-custody setup, I've learned that the barrier to Bitcoin self-custody is low — **everyone can walk into the world of Bitcoin easily and safely.**

Information security is always relative, and life is full of trade-offs. The internet is not short of Bitcoin guides, and one more scattered article only makes people more confused.  
Building a site that maintains **a "default", end-to-end workflow for the majority** — from creating, to backing up, to using a bitcoin wallet — might actually be worth something. **That is why this site exists.**
